The CX team challenge

  1. 01

    Feedback scattered across systems

    Customer signals live in support tickets, call transcripts, chat logs, NPS surveys — no single view of "what are customers telling us?" CX teams spend more time hunting for feedback than analyzing it.

  2. 02

    Themes buried in volume

    Reading through hundreds of support transcripts to find recurring complaints or feature requests is time-consuming. Without theme surfacing, CX teams rely on support team memory or anecdotal reports.

  3. 03

    Context lost in aggregation

    Traditional text analytics produce keyword counts or sentiment scores ("47 mentions of 'billing'") but don't show what customers said about billing or which conversations need attention.

How Tessavra helps CX teams

  1. 01

    Surface customer themes at scale

    Tessavra analyzes thousands of support interactions and surfaces recurring themes — product issues, feature requests, billing complaints, onboarding friction, competitive mentions — without requiring manual tagging or keyword setup.

  2. 02

    Link themes to conversation evidence

    Every theme includes a list of supporting conversation examples, with direct links to the transcript lines where customers expressed that issue. CX teams see patterns and hear the customer voice in context.

  3. 03

    Prioritize and route for action

    Themes are organized by frequency, urgency and impact. CX teams see which issues are growing, which are resolved, and which require immediate routing to product, engineering or operations teams.

VoC intelligence workflow

From conversations to customer intelligence

Tessavra turns raw customer conversations into an organized ledger of themes — each one backed by the conversations that surfaced it, so CX teams act on evidence instead of anecdotes.

  • Capture — ingest customer interactions from contact centers, CRM systems, support platforms and meeting tools — automatically, on schedule or via API
  • Analyze — conversations analyzed for recurring themes, customer pain points, feature requests, positive signals and churn risk indicators
  • Surface — themes surfaced in a VoC ledger, organized by topic, frequency and urgency, each with supporting conversation examples and evidence links
  • Act — CX teams review themes with full customer context, prioritize issues, and route feedback to product, engineering, support ops or account teams

Understanding the customer voice

Product issues

Recurring complaints about broken features, confusing workflows or technical problems. See what customers are struggling with, in their own words.

Feature requests

What customers are asking for — new capabilities, workflow improvements, integration requests. Track which requests appear most frequently and which customers are asking.

Billing & policy friction

Billing disputes, policy confusion, pricing concerns — surface where customers are frustrated with terms, pricing or account management.

Onboarding & adoption challenges

Where new customers get stuck — setup confusion, feature discovery issues, training gaps. Identify onboarding friction before it becomes churn.

Competitive mentions

When customers mention competing products or switching considerations. Understand what customers value in alternatives and why they're comparing.

Positive signals

What's working well — features customers love, successful onboarding, praise for support agents. Not just complaints.

Prioritizing and routing feedback

From themes to action

  • Prioritize by frequency and impact — see which themes appear most often, which are escalating, and which affect high-value customer segments
  • Track trends over time — monitor how themes grow, stabilize or decline, and see whether a product release, process change or training initiative resolved a recurring issue
  • Route to the right teams — share themes with product managers (feature requests), engineering (bugs), support ops (knowledge gaps) or account teams (churn risk), with supporting conversation evidence
  • Close the loop — mark themes as "investigating," "fixed" or "closed," and track when customer mentions decline after action is taken
